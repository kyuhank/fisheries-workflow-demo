MSE report

This report compares three simple catch rules under the same simulated stock conditions and observation errors.

Methods

Four fitted assessment cases supply the simulated stocks. Each rule is tested for 15 years under two recruitment scenarios, with 20 repeated trials per case and scenario. The same random errors are used when comparing rules.

Each year, an index is observed with error, a catch rule is applied, and the stock responds to that catch. The next observation comes from the updated stock. The index-based rules use a three-year mean; they do not see the true stock size.

Constant catch: Keep catch at its recent mean. Index rule: Adjust catch in proportion to the three-year mean observed index. Buffered rule: Use 80% of index-based catch advice, limiting annual advice changes to 15%.

Results

Across the three rules, mean annual catch ranged from 427 to 510 tonnes. Median final spawning biomass ranged from 0.58 to 0.61 of its unfished level.

Constant catch had average annual catch changes of 0.0%. Index rule had average annual catch changes of 6.1%. Buffered rule had average annual catch changes of 6.8%.

Interpretation

The comparison shows how management decisions feed back into future stock conditions and catches. Catch, stock condition and stability must be considered together; this example does not select a preferred rule or provide advice for a real fishery.

Scope and assumptions

Fixed biology and selectivity; no spatial structure. Future recruitment varies around a fixed scenario mean, without a stock–recruitment relationship. Index-based rules use noisy observations; no assessment is refitted during the simulations. The cases and scenarios have equal weight for comparison, not estimated probabilities.
