MSE report

This report compares three simple catch rules under the same simulated stock conditions and observation errors.

Methods

Four fitted assessment cases supply the simulated stocks. Each rule is tested for 15 years under two recruitment scenarios, with 20 repeated trials per case and scenario. The same random errors are used when comparing rules.

Each year, an index is observed with error, a catch rule is applied, and the stock responds to that catch. The next observation comes from the updated stock. The index-based rules use a three-year mean; they do not see the true stock size.

Recruitment stays at its baseline mean, or falls to half that mean for six years before recovering. Year-to-year recruitment still varies in both scenarios.

Constant catch: Keep catch at its recent mean. Index rule: Adjust catch in proportion to the three-year mean observed index. Buffered rule: Set a stepped catch target from the observed index, apply the 80% buffer, then limit annual advice changes to 15%.

Results

Across the three rules, mean annual catch ranged from 353 to 433 tonnes. Median final spawning biomass ranged from 0.68 to 0.72 of its unfished level.

Recruitment dip and recovery

Baseline recruitment

Constant catch had average annual catch changes of 0.0%. Index rule had average annual catch changes of 6.6%. Buffered rule had average annual catch changes of 4.8%.

Interpretation

The comparison shows how management decisions feed back into future stock conditions and catches. Catch, stock condition and stability must be considered together; this example does not select a preferred rule or provide advice for a real fishery.

Scope and assumptions

Fixed biology and selectivity; no spatial structure. Future recruitment varies around declared annual scenario means, without a stock–recruitment relationship. Index-based rules use noisy observations; no assessment is refitted during the simulations. The cases and scenarios have equal weight for comparison, not estimated probabilities.
